Tracker bridge

Search your private tracker from an app

Your app gets a URL and a key of its own that can only search. Your TorrentLeech login, which opens your whole account, stays on the server. Cloudflare runs the one file in between, free, and nothing is installed on your computer.

Five short steps. Each one opens another site in a new tab, so keep this page open.

  1. 1Your TorrentLeech

    Nothing is sent anywhere: this page makes no network request at all. Everything you type goes into your copy of the program and travels inside the deploy link, after the #, which browsers never send to a server.

    On TorrentLeech, open your Profile and find the RSS link. Paste the whole link if it is easier — the key is taken out of it. This is what downloads the actual .torrent, and unlike a session it does not expire.

    How it should sign in to search

    Searching needs a logged-in session; the RSS key alone cannot do it. There are two ways to give it one.

    Paste them all if it is easier. Only the ones that could be a session are kept — PHPSESSID, tluid, tlpass, cf_clearance — and the rest are dropped here, before anything is written into the file.

    Where to find them
    1. Log in to TorrentLeech in your browser, and stay logged in.
    2. Open developer tools, then Application or Storage, then Cookies, then https://www.torrentleech.org.
    3. Copy the names and values as name=value, separated by ;.

    On a phone this is awkward, and the username and password route above is the easier one there.

    On TorrentLeech: Site Profile, Alt 2FA Token. Only fill this in if you have two-factor authentication switched on; a one-time code from an app will not work here.

    One more thing, and it is ticked for a reason

    Two things worth knowing first

    A session may be tied to the address it was made at.

    A Cloudflare Worker calls from Cloudflare, not from your house, so a pasted session can be refused for that reason alone. If it is, the username and password route usually works, and running the file on your own machine always does. /healthz?probe=1 tells you which is happening.

    TorrentLeech is behind a bot filter.

    Most of the time it lets a plain request through. When it does not, it answers with a browser test that a Worker cannot take, and the bridge says so in as many words rather than returning nothing.

  2. 2Sign in to Cloudflare
    Open Cloudflare and sign in

    A free account is an email address and a password. No card, no domain. If you already have one, the same link signs you in.

    Do this before step 3. If you are signed out when you press Deploy, Cloudflare shows a login page first and the file does not survive the trip.

    That link has your TorrentLeech credentials inside it. Do not send it to anyone.

  4. 4Open it and come back

    The one thing that goes wrong

    New Cloudflare account? Wait about two minutes before you press Visit.

    Cloudflare is still issuing the certificate for your new name. Press too early and the browser shows a security warning. Nothing is broken.

    Still on Cloudflare, press the blue button at the top right.

    Your new page says Your bridge is live. Press Finish setup on it. That brings you back here with your URL filled in.

    It says the connection is not secure

    Normal on a new account: the certificate for your new workers.dev name is not ready yet. Wait a minute and press Visit again. If it keeps happening, open the Domains tab on your Worker, switch on the Preview URL, and press Visit once more.

    Either way, that Domains tab shows your URL at the top. Copy it, press the button below, and paste it in. Your settings are already inside the Worker.

  5. 5Your URL and key

    Your URL arrived. Your key is under it. Copy both somewhere you will still have next week.

    This browser no longer has the key that went into this bridge. Saved keys are dropped after a week, and another browser never had it.

    It is not lost. Open the Worker in Cloudflare, press Edit code, and read the line near the top that starts const BRIDGE_KEY. The key shown below is a new one and belongs to no bridge.

What you are deploying



  One file, which Cloudflare runs at a URL of its own. It takes a search from
  your app, asks TorrentLeech as you, reads enough of each result to identify
  it, and hands the list back. Cloudflare calls this a Worker.



    
  
  • It only searches. Four routes, all read-only. Nothing it can do changes anything in your account: it cannot upload, cannot post, cannot delete, and cannot spend your ratio.
    • 
  
  • Your tracker sign-in never reaches your app. Your app gets a key that can only search, and you can change it without touching TorrentLeech.
    • 
  
  • Nothing runs on your computer. No app, no extension, no download.
    • 
  
  • There is nothing to bill you for. The free plan covers 100,000 requests a day, and no card is involved.
    • 
  
  • Nobody else gets your URL. There is no public copy of this and no list of other people's. The only one that exists is the one you make.
    •

How to delete it



  In Cloudflare, open Workers & Pages, click your Worker, then
  Settings, then Delete. The URL stops answering at once. If
  you would also like the session it held to stop working, log out of
  TorrentLeech once, which ends it everywhere.




What it does not do



  
Not a search engineTorrentLeech does the searching. What it has, and what it shows you, is its business and yours.

  
Not anonymityTorrentLeech sees the query, and on this path Cloudflare carries it. No log is kept here, but that is a statement about logging and nothing more.

  
Not a clientIt hosts nothing, stores nothing and transfers no file to anywhere but you. It returns names, sizes, swarm counts and links.

  
Not a way around a ratioEvery .torrent it hands over is one your own account asked for, with your own passkey in it. Your ratio is yours.
